Getting there, via London

The Mayfair Townhouse, 27–41 Half Moon Street, W1J 7BG · 1 night

London tips

  • Heathrow to Mayfair: an Uber or black cab takes 45–75 minutes depending on traffic. The Elizabeth line to Bond Street plus a 5-minute taxi is usually faster and about a quarter of the price.
  • Getting around: tap a contactless credit card on the Tube and buses — no ticket needed. Same card in and out. Green Park station is a two-minute walk from the hotel.
  • Theatre: the TKTS booth on Leicester Square sells same-day discounted seats from about 10am. Most West End shows also start at 7:30pm, so a 6pm dinner nearby works.
  • Money: almost everywhere takes cards. Restaurants often add a 12.5% service charge — check the bill before tipping again.
  • Jet lag: you land at 1pm. Stay outside in daylight (Green Park and Hyde Park are next door) and aim to stay up until at least 9pm.
  • Plugs: UK type G (three flat pins). Georgia, Azerbaijan and Türkiye all use the round two-pin European type.

Tbilisi, Georgia

Rooms Hotel Tbilisi · Sept 17–20 and Sept 22

Tbilisi tips

  • Entry: US passport holders don't need a visa for Georgia. Tbilisi is 4 hours ahead of London, 9 hours ahead of Austin.
  • Money: the currency is the lari (GEL). Cards are common in the city; carry some cash for markets, taxis and small cafés. ATMs are everywhere.
  • Getting around: download the Bolt app before you go — it's the local Uber and it's cheap. Avoid flagging cabs on the street without agreeing a price.
  • Don't miss: the Old Town and the sulfur baths of Abanotubani, the cable car up to Narikala Fortress, the funicular to Mtatsminda Park for the view, and the Dry Bridge flea market on the weekend.
  • Eating: khinkali (soup dumplings) are eaten by hand — hold the knot, bite, sip the broth, and leave the knot on the plate. Khachapuri (cheese bread) is the other must. Georgian dinners come with long toasts; sip along, no need to finish each glass.
  • Water: bottled water is cheap and everywhere — the easy choice while travelling.
  • Wine tour (Sept 19): Georgia's wine is made in buried clay vessels called qvevri, which is what you'll see in the Kakheti region. Bring a hat and sunscreen; it's a long, warm day out.

Kazbegi, the Caucasus mountains

Rooms Hotel Kazbegi · Sept 20–22

Kazbegi tips

  • The drive: about 3–4 hours along the Georgian Military Highway. Ask the driver to stop at the Ananuri fortress on the reservoir and at the Gudauri viewpoint (the colourful Soviet-era mural) — both are on the way.
  • Pack warm: the town sits at around 1,750m and evenings in late September are cold, even if Tbilisi is warm. Bring a jacket, closed shoes and something for rain.
  • The view: the hotel terrace faces Mount Kazbek (5,000m+). Mornings are usually the clearest — coffee outside before breakfast is the moment.
  • Gergeti Trinity Church: the famous church on the hill above town. You can hike up (2 hours, steep) or take a 4x4 taxi from the town square for a small fare — the hotel can arrange it. Women may be asked to cover shoulders and hair; wraps are provided at the door.
  • Take it easy: the altitude is mild but noticeable; drink water and don't rush the first afternoon.
  • Cash: there are a couple of ATMs in town, but not many — get lari in Tbilisi before you leave.

Baku, Azerbaijan

Shah Palace Hotel · Sept 23–27 · 4 nights, paid in full

Baku tips

  • Visa: US citizens need an Azerbaijan e-visa before arrival — make sure you have the printed or saved approval with you at the airport in Tbilisi.
  • Money: the manat (AZN). Cards work in hotels, restaurants and malls; cash for taxis and the bazaar. Bolt works here too.
  • Race week: it's a street circuit, so roads around downtown and the seafront close for the weekend. Walk where you can, leave well ahead of session times, and expect security checks at the gates. Bring ear protection, sunscreen, a hat and a refillable bottle. Your seats are in the Absheron grandstand, Section C (upper), valid Thursday to Saturday.
  • Between sessions: the walled Old City (İçərişəhər) with the Maiden Tower and Shirvanshahs' Palace is a short stroll from the hotel; the Baku Boulevard along the Caspian is lovely at sunset; the Heydar Aliyev Center (the curvy white building) is worth a taxi ride; the Flame Towers light up at night.
  • Eating: try plov (saffron rice), dolma, kebabs and pomegranate everything. Tea comes in pear-shaped glasses with jam on the side — that's the local ritual.
  • Etiquette: Baku is relaxed, but dress a little more conservatively away from the seafront and don't photograph government buildings.

Istanbul, Türkiye

The Wings Hotels Karaköy, Kemankeş Mah. Gümrük Sok. No. 26, Beyoğlu · 1 night

Istanbul tips

  • Two airports — this matters: you fly into Sabiha Gökçen (SAW) on the Asian side on Sunday, but fly out of Istanbul Airport (IST) on the European side on Monday. They're about 1.5 hours apart. Don't mix them up.
  • SAW to the hotel: a taxi takes 1–1.5 hours; the Havabus shuttle to Taksim then a short taxi is cheaper. Karaköy is right by the Galata Bridge.
  • Monday's timing is tight: the tour starts at 9:00am at the German Fountain in Sultanahmet (the old-city square between Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque). Your flight leaves IST at 6:00pm and IST is 45–90 minutes from the centre — leave the tour or the hotel by 2:30pm at the latest. Ask the hotel to hold your bags in the morning and book a taxi in advance.
  • Money: Turkish lira (TRY). Cards are widely accepted; carry small cash for tips, tea and street food. Tipping 5–10% in restaurants is normal.
  • Mosques: free to enter outside prayer times. Cover shoulders and knees; women cover their hair (scarves are lent at the door). Shoes come off.
  • Near the hotel: Galata Tower, the Karaköy fish sandwich boats, and the ferry to Kadıköy on the Asian side — a 20-minute ride with the best skyline view in the city.
  • Getting around: the tram (T1) runs from Karaköy straight to Sultanahmet in about 10 minutes; buy an Istanbulkart at any station kiosk or tap a contactless card.

İzmir and the Aegean coast

LaVista Boutique Hotel & Spa · Sept 28 – Oct 1 · 3 nights

İzmir tips

  • The airport: İzmir Adnan Menderes (ADB). Taxis are metered; the Havaş shuttle and the İZBAN train also run into the city. Check with the hotel whether they offer a transfer.
  • Ephesus: the big one — among the best-preserved ancient cities anywhere, about an hour south. Go early or late to beat the heat, wear good shoes (marble is slippery) and pay the extra for the Terrace Houses. A driver-guide for the day is the easiest way; the hotel can book one.
  • Also nearby: Şirince, a hillside wine village near Ephesus; Alaçatı, a stone town of boutiques and restaurants on the Çeşme peninsula; and Ilıca beach for a swim — late September water is still warm.
  • In the city: walk the Kordon waterfront at sunset, ride the elevator (Asansör) for the view, and browse the Kemeraltı bazaar.
  • Eating: this is Türkiye's meze and seafood coast. Order a spread of small plates, grilled fish, and the local boyoz pastry at breakfast. Kumru is İzmir's own sandwich.
  • Spa days: the hotel has a spa — book treatments the day you arrive since slots go quickly.

Cappadocia

Seraphim Cave Hotel & Spa · Oct 1–3 · €734 including airport transfers

Cappadocia tips

  • Your driver: a private transfer is arranged. The driver waits at the arrivals exit with a sign with your name on it. Pickup is for Turkish Airlines flight TK2086.
  • Hot-air balloons: the thing everyone comes for. They fly at sunrise (pickup around 5am) and only if the wind allows, so book through the hotel for your first morning and keep the second as a backup. Dress warmly — it's cold in the basket before dawn.
  • Cave hotel nights are chilly: early October mornings can drop near 5°C. Ask for extra blankets if you need them.
  • Your €400 hotel tour (Oct 2): a private day typically covers the Göreme Open Air Museum (rock-cut churches with frescoes), an underground city such as Derinkuyu, Uçhisar Castle for the view, and the valleys. Wear walking shoes with grip.
  • Sunset: Red Valley or the viewpoint above Göreme — ask the hotel for the best spot that day.
  • Departure transfer: the hotel will confirm your pickup time the day before you leave, but they asked you to remind them — do it on Oct 2. Flight TK2003 leaves at 10:20am on Oct 3.
  • Souvenirs: Avanos is the pottery town; carpets are lovely but only buy if you've done your homework on price.

London, then home

The Hoxton, Holborn · Oct 3 · 1 night

Tips for the last two days

  • Oct 3 connection in Istanbul: you have about 2 hours between landing from Nevşehir and the London flight. Both are Turkish Airlines, so bags should go straight through — check at Nevşehir that they're tagged to LHR.
  • Heathrow to Holborn: the Elizabeth line to Tottenham Court Road, then a 10-minute walk or short taxi, is the quickest. Check-in opens at 2pm; you'll land at 4pm so it lines up.
  • Holborn evening: the British Museum (free, open late on Fridays only, so aim for a morning visit Oct 4 before checkout at noon), Covent Garden and its restaurants are a 10-minute walk.
  • Oct 4 at Heathrow: Delta flies from Terminal 3. Be there by 12:30pm for the 3:15pm departure.
  • Atlanta layover is 1 hour 41 minutes: you clear US immigration and customs in Atlanta, collect your bags, re-drop them, and go back through security. Use Mobile Passport Control or Global Entry if you have it, and don't dawdle. Your Austin flight is the last of the day.
  • Seats: 30F and 30G on the London–Atlanta flight, 11C and 11A Atlanta–Austin, both Delta Comfort.
